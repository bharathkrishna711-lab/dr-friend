# Disease: UTI
## Category: Infectious
## Source: NHS - Urinary Tract Infections (nhs.uk)

## Overview
A urinary tract infection (UTI) is a bacterial infection affecting any
part of the urinary system, most commonly the bladder. UTIs are common,
especially in women, and are generally treatable, but can become serious
if the infection spreads to the kidneys.

## Common Symptoms
- Burning sensation during urination
- Frequent urge to urinate, often with little urine passed
- Cloudy, strong-smelling, or bloody urine
- Lower abdominal or pelvic discomfort
- Mild fever in some cases

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Bacteria entering the urinary tract
- More common in women due to anatomy
- Sexual activity
- Poor hydration
- Urinary catheter use

## Self-Care Guidance
- Drink plenty of water to help flush bacteria
- Urinate frequently and avoid holding urine for long periods
- Maintain good personal hygiene
- Avoid irritants like caffeine and alcohol during infection

## When to See a Doctor
- Burning sensation or frequent urination lasting more than a day
- Blood in urine
- Symptoms not improving with increased fluid intake

## When It's an Emergency
- Fever with chills alongside urinary symptoms (possible kidney
  involvement)
- Severe back or side pain
- Nausea and vomiting with urinary symptoms
- Confusion, particularly in older adults
