# Disease: Anxiety Attack
## Category: Neurological
## Source: NHS - Generalised Anxiety Disorder (nhs.uk)

## Overview
An anxiety attack (often linked to panic disorder or generalised anxiety)
is a sudden episode of intense fear or worry accompanied by physical
symptoms. Episodes can feel alarming but are not typically physically
dangerous, though symptoms can closely mimic cardiac conditions.

## Common Symptoms
- Rapid heartbeat or palpitations
- Chest tightness or discomfort
- Shortness of breath or feeling smothered
- Sweating, trembling, or shaking
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Feeling of impending doom or loss of control
- Nausea or stomach discomfort

## Causes and Risk Factors
- History of anxiety disorders
- High stress or major life changes
- Family history of anxiety
- Excessive caffeine or stimulant use
- Sleep deprivation

## Self-Care Guidance
- Practice slow, deep breathing techniques
- Ground yourself using sensory awareness (5 senses technique)
- Reduce caffeine and stimulant intake
- Maintain regular sleep and exercise routines
- Consider mindfulness or relaxation practices

## When to See a Doctor
- Frequent or recurring anxiety attacks
- Symptoms interfering with daily life or work
- Difficulty controlling worry most days

## When It's an Emergency
- Chest pain that is severe, crushing, or radiating to the arm/jaw
  (cannot be safely assumed to be anxiety without medical evaluation)
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- Severe difficulty breathing not improving with calming techniques
