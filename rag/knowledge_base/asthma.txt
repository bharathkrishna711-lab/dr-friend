# Disease: Asthma
## Category: Respiratory
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Asthma (who.int)

## Overview
Asthma is a chronic condition affecting the airways in the lungs, causing
them to narrow, swell, and produce extra mucus. This makes breathing
difficult and can trigger coughing, wheezing, and shortness of breath.
Symptoms often come in episodes ("attacks") rather than being constant.

## Common Symptoms
- Shortness of breath
- Wheezing (a whistling sound when breathing)
- Chest tightness
- Coughing, especially at night or early morning
- Symptoms triggered or worsened by exercise, cold air, or allergens

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Family history of asthma or allergies
- Exposure to allergens (dust, pollen, pet dander)
- Respiratory infections, especially in childhood
- Air pollution and occupational irritants
- Obesity

## Self-Care Guidance
- Use prescribed inhalers as directed
- Identify and avoid personal triggers
- Monitor symptoms and peak flow if advised by a doctor
- Maintain a smoke-free environment
- Keep rescue medication accessible at all times

## When to See a Doctor
- Symptoms occurring more than twice a week
- Needing rescue inhaler frequently
- Symptoms interfering with sleep or daily activities

## When It's an Emergency
- Severe shortness of breath not relieved by inhaler
- Difficulty speaking full sentences due to breathlessness
- Bluish tint to lips or fingernails
- Rapid worsening of symptoms despite medication
