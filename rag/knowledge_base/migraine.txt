# Disease: Migraine
## Category: Neurological
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Headache Disorders (who.int)

## Overview
Migraine is a neurological condition causing recurrent, often severe
headaches, typically on one side of the head. It is one of the most common
neurological disorders worldwide and can significantly affect daily
functioning during episodes.

## Common Symptoms
- Throbbing or pulsing headache, often one-sided
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia) and sound (phonophobia)
- Nausea, sometimes with vomiting
- Visual disturbances (aura) in some cases before headache onset
- Worsening of pain with physical activity

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Family history of migraine
- Hormonal changes (more common in women)
- Stress, poor sleep, or irregular meals
- Certain foods, caffeine, or alcohol as triggers
- Bright lights or strong smells

## Self-Care Guidance
- Rest in a quiet, dark room during an attack
- Stay hydrated and maintain regular meal times
- Identify and avoid personal triggers where possible
- Over-the-counter pain relief (paracetamol/ibuprofen) for mild episodes
- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule

## When to See a Doctor
- Migraines occurring more than a few times a month
- Symptoms not responding to usual self-care measures
- Migraine significantly interfering with work or daily life

## When It's an Emergency
- Sudden, "worst headache of your life" (may indicate a different condition)
- Headache with confusion, slurred speech, or weakness on one side
- Headache following a head injury
- Headache with stiff neck and fever (possible meningitis)
