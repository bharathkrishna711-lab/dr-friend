# Disease: Tuberculosis
## Category: Respiratory
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Tuberculosis (who.int)

## Overview
Tuberculosis (TB) is a bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium
tuberculosis, most commonly affecting the lungs. It spreads through the
air when an infected person coughs or sneezes. India carries one of the
highest TB burdens globally, making early recognition important.

## Common Symptoms
- Persistent cough lasting more than 2-3 weeks, sometimes with blood
- Chest pain
- Unintentional weight loss
- Night sweats
- Fever, often low-grade and prolonged
- Fatigue and loss of appetite

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Close contact with someone with active TB
- Weakened immune system (HIV, diabetes, malnutrition)
- Living in crowded or poorly ventilated conditions
- Previous TB infection

## Self-Care Guidance
- Complete the full prescribed course of TB medication, even after
  feeling better -- incomplete treatment leads to drug resistance
- Cover mouth when coughing and maintain good ventilation
- Maintain adequate nutrition to support recovery
- Attend all follow-up appointments for monitoring

## When to See a Doctor
- Cough lasting more than 2-3 weeks
- Unexplained weight loss with prolonged low-grade fever
- Coughing up blood

## When It's an Emergency
- Coughing up significant amounts of blood
- Severe difficulty breathing
- High fever with confusion
- Severe chest pain
