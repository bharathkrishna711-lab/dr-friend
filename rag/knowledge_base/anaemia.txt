# Disease: Anaemia
## Category: Metabolic
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Anaemia (who.int)

## Overview
Anaemia occurs when the blood does not have enough healthy red blood
cells or haemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to the body's tissues. It
is one of the most common blood disorders in India, often linked to
nutritional deficiency, particularly iron deficiency.

## Common Symptoms
- Fatigue and weakness
- Pale skin
- Shortness of breath, especially with exertion
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Cold hands and feet
- Headache

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Iron, vitamin B12, or folate deficiency
- Chronic blood loss (heavy menstrual periods, gastrointestinal bleeding)
- Pregnancy
- Chronic diseases affecting red blood cell production
- Poor dietary intake

## Self-Care Guidance
- Eat iron-rich foods (leafy greens, legumes, meat if applicable)
- Pair iron-rich foods with vitamin C to improve absorption
- Take iron or vitamin supplements only as advised by a doctor
- Get adequate rest

## When to See a Doctor
- Persistent fatigue or weakness affecting daily activities
- Noticeable pale skin or nail beds
- Shortness of breath with mild exertion

## When It's an Emergency
- Severe shortness of breath at rest
- Chest pain
- Rapid heartbeat with dizziness or fainting
- Signs of significant blood loss 

## Typical Lab Findings
- Low haemoglobin (Hb), below the normal range for age and sex
- Low haematocrit (PCV), reflecting reduced red blood cell volume
- Low red blood cell count (RBC) in many cases
- Mean corpuscular volume (MCV) may be low (iron deficiency), normal, or
  high (vitamin B12/folate deficiency) depending on the underlying cause
