# Disease: Dengue Fever
## Category: Infectious
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Dengue and Severe Dengue (who.int)

## Overview
Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection transmitted primarily by the
Aedes aegypti mosquito. It is common in tropical and subtropical regions,
including much of India, particularly during and after monsoon season when
stagnant water increases mosquito breeding.

## Common Symptoms
- Sudden high fever
- Severe headache, often behind the eyes
- Muscle and joint pain
- Nausea and vomiting
- Skin rash
- Mild bleeding (nose or gums) in some cases

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Bite from an infected Aedes mosquito
- Living in or traveling to areas with standing water and high mosquito density
- Previous dengue infection (increases risk of severe dengue on reinfection)

## Self-Care Guidance
- Rest and stay well hydrated with water or oral rehydration solution
- Use paracetamol for fever and pain (avoid aspirin or ibuprofen, which
  increase bleeding risk)
- Monitor temperature and symptoms closely for the first week
- Use mosquito nets/repellent to prevent further bites during illness

## When to See a Doctor 
- Fever lasting more than 2 days without improvement
- Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down
- Severe abdominal pain

## When It's an Emergency
- Bleeding from gums, nose, or in vomit/stool
- Difficulty breathing or severe restlessness
- Cold, clammy skin or rapid weak pulse (signs of shock)
- Sudden drop in temperature after high fever combined with any of the above

## Typical Lab Findings
- Low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) -- often the most notable and
  earliest abnormal finding
- Elevated haematocrit (haemoconcentration), particularly as illness
  progresses or plasma leaks in more severe cases
- Low white blood cell count (leukopenia), especially in the first few
  days of illness
- Mildly to moderately elevated liver enzymes (SGPT/ALT, SGOT/AST) are
  common findings
