# Disease: Hypertensive Crisis
## Category: Cardiac
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Cardiovascular Diseases (who.int)

## Overview
A hypertensive crisis is a severe, sudden increase in blood pressure
that can lead to organ damage if not treated promptly. It is considered
a serious cardiac event requiring urgent medical attention.

## Common Symptoms
- Severe headache
- Blurred or impaired vision
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Nosebleed
- Severe anxiety or confusion
- Nausea and vomiting

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Uncontrolled or untreated high blood pressure
- Missed doses of blood pressure medication
- Kidney disease
- Stress or significant physical exertion
- Certain medications or substances

## Self-Care Guidance
- Take prescribed blood pressure medication consistently
- Monitor blood pressure regularly if previously diagnosed with
  hypertension
- Reduce sodium intake and maintain a heart-healthy diet
- Avoid sudden intense physical exertion if hypertensive

## When to See a Doctor
- Blood pressure readings significantly above your normal baseline
- Recurring headaches with high blood pressure history

## When It's an Emergency
- Severe headache with blurred vision
- Chest pain or shortness of breath with high blood pressure
- Confusion, difficulty speaking, or weakness on one side
- Blood pressure reading at or above 180/120 mmHg with any symptoms
