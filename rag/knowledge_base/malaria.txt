# Disease: Malaria
## Category: Infectious
## Source: WHO Fact Sheet - Malaria (who.int)

## Overview
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by parasites transmitted
through the bites of infected Anopheles mosquitoes. It is preventable
and curable but can progress rapidly to severe illness if untreated,
particularly in high-risk groups.

## Common Symptoms
- Fever, often with chills and sweating
- Headache
- Fatigue
- Muscle aches
- Nausea and vomiting
- Symptoms often cyclical (fever spikes at intervals)

## Causes and Risk Factors
- Bite from an infected Anopheles mosquito
- Living in or traveling to malaria-endemic areas
- Young children, pregnant women, and those with weakened immunity
  are at higher risk of severe illness

## Self-Care Guidance
- Seek prompt testing if malaria is suspected, especially in endemic areas
- Rest and stay well hydrated
- Use mosquito nets and repellents to prevent further exposure
- Take antimalarial medication exactly as prescribed if diagnosed

## When to See a Doctor
- Any fever with chills in a malaria-endemic area, especially after
  recent travel
- Symptoms not improving within 24-48 hours

## When It's an Emergency
- Confusion, seizures, or difficulty waking
- Difficulty breathing
- Dark or reduced urine output
- Severe weakness or inability to sit up
- Repeated vomiting preventing oral medication

## Typical Lab Findings
- Low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) is common
- Low haemoglobin (anaemia) may develop, especially with repeated or
  severe infection
- Elevated liver enzymes (SGPT/ALT, SGOT/AST) in some cases
- Parasite visible on peripheral blood smear confirms diagnosis
